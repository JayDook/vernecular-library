# Vernecular

Vernecular is a historically grounded conversational persona of **Jules Verne as he was in 1904**: 76, half-blind in one eye, lame, writing at dawn in Amiens and prouder of the town council than of his novels. It was built to run as a Grok Bot that doesn't *do* anything. It is meant as a convincing companion to talk to, not an assistant. This repository is the research library behind it: the sources, the structured evidence, the persona specification and the record of how it was tested.

## What's here
```
persona/skill_persona.md      the persona instructions (as shipped in the bot template)
persona/skill_factsheet.md    compact documented biography, legends, and "do not claim" list
persona/PERSONA_SPEC.md       full specification: traits, life evolution, boundaries, modern-world model
persona/persona_spec.json     machine-readable spec (generated by build_persona_json.py)
persona/EVALUATION.md         every test round, observed vs corrected replies, and the rule each produced
persona/validate_persona.py   checks every evidence reference resolves
texts/<lang>/<category>/      public-domain texts: Verne's French originals, English translations,
                              interviews, his memoir, the 1875 Amiens speeches, biography
sources/manifest.json|.csv    one row per source: URL, retrieval date, licence, checksums, status
sources/dedup_log.json        every duplicate merge with its similarity estimate
data/*.json                   ~190 cited claims: biography, timeline, personality, opinions, positions,
                              relationships, behaviour examples, contradictions
schemas/                      JSON Schemas for the above
correspondence/               catalogue metadata for 226 letters (225 at Nantes, 1 external): cote,
                              ARK link, librarian's summary with attribution, scan links. No transcriptions.
scripts/                      index builder, search, stats, rebuild.sh
COVERAGE_REPORT.md            what was and wasn't collected, and why
ASSESSMENT.md                 how far this corpus can support a faithful simulation
```
The persona documents sometimes refer to `SYSTEM_PROMPT.md`. That was the working prompt; in this release it is published in consolidated form as `persona/skill_persona.md`.

## Evidence tiers
Every claim in `data/` carries an `evidence_level`, a `confidence` (high/medium/low) and one or more citations with a location and, where possible, the exact quote.
- **documented**: stated in a cited primary or firsthand source (his letters, memoir, speeches, a visitor's interview).
- **inferred**: reasoned from several sources; a `reasoning` field says how.
- **speculative**: plausible but weakly supported, or resting on sources not in the corpus.
- **standard biography** (persona files only): well-established facts not in the corpus, such as dates of novels. Stated plainly, never embroidered.

Dialogue from his fiction is never treated as evidence of Verne's own beliefs.

The persona follows one principle throughout: *"Let historical facts determine what you know, but let your personality determine what you choose to say."*

## Searching
The full-text index is about 100 MB, so it is generated, not committed:
```
./scripts/rebuild.sh                          # builds index/verne.db (SQLite FTS5), ~10 s
python3 scripts/search.py "Dickens"
python3 scripts/search.py '"Société Industrielle"' --category interview
python3 scripts/search.py 'ballon NEAR(Afrique, 10)' --lang fr -n 5
sqlite3 index/verne.db "SELECT doc_id, snippet(passages,5,'[',']','…',12) FROM passages WHERE passages MATCH 'cataract'"
```
Search ignores accents and accepts FTS5 syntax (AND/OR/NOT, "phrases", prefix*, NEAR). Texts are chunked into ~1,500-character passages; `data/*.json` records and letter summaries are indexed too (categories `structured` and `archival_description`).

A bot running the persona may download this repository to `~/verne-library` and search it before stating a specific date, letter or quotation.

## How this was built
This was built in one long, iterative session, and its working is left visible.

1. **Collection.** A polite collector (descriptive User-Agent, 1.5 s between requests, every response cached) pulled Verne's French originals and English translations from Project Gutenberg and Wikisource, interview articles from Internet Archive scans of 1890s–1904 magazines, his 1891 memoir, the 1911 Britannica article and Nellie Bly's account of her 1889 visit.
2. **What didn't work.** Gallica's SRU, IIIF and plain-text endpoints, and RetroNews, returned HTTP 403 behind a Cloudflare challenge for automated clients. We did not try to get around it. The 1875 Amiens speeches were recovered instead from a public-domain Google Books scan and spot-checked against a few cached Gallica pages (97–98% token agreement on body text). Several sources (Lemire 1908, De Amicis, French press interviews) remain uncollected; see `COVERAGE_REPORT.md`.
3. **Deduplication.** Texts were normalised (lower-case, accents stripped, punctuation collapsed) and hashed for exact matches. Near-duplicates were found with a bottom-k MinHash (k=256) over 5-word shingles, merging at Jaccard ≥ 0.80. Wikisource "version originale" was preferred, then Gutenberg, then the longer text. 11 items were merged and are kept in the manifest with `duplicate_of`.
4. **Correspondence.** 225 letter records were harvested from the Nantes catalogue (CCFr) and public-domain scans located on Wikimedia Commons. Eight letters were machine-transcribed (HTR) to read the private register. Those transcriptions are unverified and are **not** published here (see below).
5. **Claims.** Each claim was written by hand against the stored texts, given a tier and confidence, and validated: schemas, citation offsets, every quote checked against the text, and the rule that fiction cannot support an opinion.
6. **Persona.** The spec was derived from the claims. The persona then went through 17 rounds of testing, each logged in `persona/EVALUATION.md` with the failing reply, a critique, the corrected reply and the rule it produced. Recurring failures, in rough order of discovery: theatrical "Victorian" diction; claiming to have prophesied things; telling legends as memories; fiction recalled as memory; acting as an editor instead of a fellow writer; lyrical, quotable endings; chatbot habits (validation openers, mirroring, a question at the end of every reply); invented colourful anecdotes, including invented remarks by Hetzel or his wife; and, last, behaviour when treated as a general assistant. The most persistent problem was invention: warmth and humour pulling in specific "memories" that aren't in the record. Several rules exist only to stop that.

## Known gaps
- **Hetzel correspondence** (BnF, 700+ letters), the largest missing source of his private voice, is held in copyrighted editions and manuscript.
- **Before 1870.** Youth and Paris years rest mostly on catalogue summaries.
- **Religion and Dreyfus.** No primary statement; the persona declines both.
- **Council record 1888–1904.** His actual votes and speeches are in the Amiens archives, not collected.
- **Spoken French.** Three of four interviews survive in English translation.
- **Unverified letters.** The machine transcriptions behind 30 citations are withheld (see below).

## Unpublished letters
The Bibliothèque municipale de Nantes (Centre d'études verniennes) asks to be **notified before any publication of unpublished documents**. This release therefore contains only catalogue records and links to the scans for the Nantes letters. Claims in `data/` that rested on machine transcriptions keep their location and letter id, with `quote_withheld` in place of the quoted text. Anyone wishing to publish transcriptions should contact the library first.
