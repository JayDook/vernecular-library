## Missing (priority order)
1. **Correspondence. This is the largest gap: zero letters collected.** The ~700 Verne–Hetzel letters and the letters to his father Pierre and mother Sophie (1848–1870s) exist only in copyrighted modern critical editions (Dumas, Gondolo della Riva and Dehs, Slatkine 1999–2006; Dumas, *Lettres à sa famille*). The letters themselves are PD, but no PD transcription was found on Wikisource, Gutenberg or IA. Without them, opinions on politics, religion, money, Hetzel's editing and his marriage rest on interviews or on secondary sources.
2. **Other contemporary interviews.** Not collected: Edmondo De Amicis (*Nuovi ritratti letterari e artistici*, 1908; PD, but no digital text found; Gutenberg's *Ritratti letterari* was checked and has no Verne material), Gordon Jones (*Temple Bar*, 1904), Adolphe Brisson (*Portraits intimes*, 1899), Charles Dawbarn (1905), and the French press interviews in Compère & Margot's *Entretiens* (1998, a copyrighted compilation whose originals are in Gallica newspapers).
3. **Charles Lemire, *Jules Verne* (1908).** PD, but no plain text was located. Try Gallica full-text search or HathiTrust.
4. **Verne's memoir "Souvenirs d'enfance et de jeunesse" (c. 1890; English version in *Youth's Companion*, 1891).** Very high value for his own voice. Not located.
5. **Marguerite Allotte de la Fuÿe (1928).** PD status is uncertain, and the book is known to embellish facts. Use with caution if it is added.
6. **Nonfiction and essays.** Missing: "Edgard Poë et ses œuvres" (1864), "Les Méridiens et le calendrier", the Amiens academy speeches (e.g. "Une ville idéale", 1875), and the municipal reports. Les voyageurs du XIXe siècle is only partly transcribed. The original manuscript of Wilhelm Storitz has not been transcribed. *Paris au XXe siècle* is copyrighted (published 1994).
7. **Historical and scientific context, 1828–1905.** Not collected beyond what the novels themselves contain. Good PD candidates: Flammarion's *Astronomie populaire*, Figuier, Tissandier's *La Nature*, Reclus' *Nouvelle géographie universelle* (Verne names these as his reading), and histories of the Commune and the Third Republic.
8. **Missing items.** Gutenberg #10547 returned 404 and was skipped. Several items in Verne's Wikisource catalogue were not fetched, including Michel Strogoff (1881 play version), Kéraban, and the posthumous originals of La Chasse au météore and the Phare.

## Known limitations
- The interview texts are uncorrected OCR (e.g. "T was born" for "I was born"). Quotes keep the OCR spellings. Page locations are approximate, but char offsets are exact.
- Interviews were given to English-language audiences through interpreters, so his warmth toward Britain and America may be tailored. They also contain factual errors, which are flagged in `caveats`.
- Firsthand sources cover mostly 1883–1895. Material on his youth comes almost entirely from his own late recollections.
- Politics (the 1888 municipal list, Dreyfus) and religion are only inferred or speculative.

## Most valuable further research
1. **Hetzel correspondence and family letters.** Use library copies of the Slatkine volumes for paraphrased, cited claims (not text reuse), or find PD transcriptions in the *Bulletin de la Société Jules Verne* where the copyright has lapsed.
2. **Gallica press, 1863–1905.** Le Figaro, Le Temps, the Journal d'Amiens and Le Progrès de la Somme carry original French interviews, his municipal speeches and the election lists. This is the best primary source for his politics and his spoken French register.
3. **Amiens archives** (Bibliothèque municipale, which holds the manuscripts and the municipal council minutes).
4. **De Amicis, Lemire and the *Youth's Companion* memoir.** All PD and all high value. Digitising or OCR-ing them would fill the gaps on his voice and childhood.
5. **Nantes Musée Jules Verne and Centre international Jules Verne** for photographs, inventories and his library catalogue, which would back up his reading claims.
